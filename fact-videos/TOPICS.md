## A. Everyday apps & India facts
- **Uber and low phone battery** (PLANNED 23 Oct). Verified: in 2016 Keith Chen, then Uber's head of economic research, said on NPR's Hidden Brain that remaining battery is one of the strongest predictors of how readily people accept surge pricing, and that Uber "absolutely [doesn't] use that to push you a higher surge price". Uber has since said it does not use battery level and that pricing comes from demand and driver supply (one statement called the claim "100% false"). A small 2023 test by Belgian paper Derniere Heure saw a 6% higher fare on a 12%-battery phone, but repeats failed and Snopes rates it "unproven". Frame as: the psychology is real, there is NO proof Uber uses it against you. Never say "Uber charges you more". End on a neutral question (charge the phone before booking or not?).
- **Google Maps and 99 phones**: artist Simon Weckert (Berlin, Feb 2020) pulled 99 second-hand phones in a handcart and made Google Maps show a traffic jam on empty streets, because Maps infers traffic from many slow-moving phones. Source: Guardian / news reports of his project. Check Google's response before claiming more.

## B. Impress-people psychology tricks (state as "researchers found", never "always works")
- **Ben Franklin effect**: asking someone for a small favour can make them like you more (Jecker & Landy, 1969).
- **Pratfall effect**: a competent person who makes a small blunder becomes more likable (Aronson, Willerman & Floyd, 1966).
- **Foot-in-the-door**: agreeing to a small request raises the odds of agreeing to a bigger one (Freedman & Fraser, 1966).
- **Chameleon effect** (mimicry, Chartrand & Bargh, 1999): check replication status before using.
