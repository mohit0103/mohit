# Build Jarvis from components, not from scratch

As of October 2026, no single product or open-source project delivers the full loop you want: an assistant that phones you every morning, remembers what you tell it, and calls back to ask "how was the dentist?". All the parts exist, though, and you can assemble a polished single-user version in roughly three months. The recommended build has four parts. First, a **cascaded voice pipeline on Pipecat or LiveKit Agents, hosted in Mumbai**, using Sarvam for Hinglish speech recognition and Indian voices, with a Plivo India number for real phone calls. Second, a **durable scheduler (Temporal or a Postgres-backed queue)** that owns every morning call, night-before alert and follow-up. Third, a **hybrid memory**: a small profile that is always loaded, plus a dated episodic log and a temporal knowledge graph (Graphiti or Mem0). Fourth, and most important, a **separate, code-managed "intentions" store**. That store is what produces the dentist follow-up. Research shows frontier LLMs manage only about 65% F1 at remembering to act in the future when left to themselves, but reach about 83% when a typed state machine tracks the intentions. OpenClaw (MIT, about 391k stars) is the closest existing open-source system and is worth studying or forking for its heartbeat and voice-call plugin. Running costs for one user come to roughly ₹1.5–3 per call-minute, or a few hundred rupees a month. The hard problems are not cost. They are getting the timing of proactive calls right, avoiding creepiness, and defending against prompt injection hidden in your own inbox.

## OpenClaw comes closest, but the dentist follow-up is unbuilt everywhere

Among open-source projects, **OpenClaw** is the nearest match to "Jarvis". It has a "heartbeat" loop that lets the agent start contact during configured active hours, cron-style skills, a Google Workspace CLI (`gogcli`), and an official voice-call plugin. The plugin places outbound calls and accepts inbound ones through Twilio, Telnyx or Plivo, and supports full-duplex realtime voice and streaming transcription ([OpenClaw voice-call docs](https://docs.openclaw.ai/plugins/voice-call); [Hyperight](https://hyperight.com/openclaws-proactive-ai-agents-are-messaging-humans-and-organizing-themselves/)). It carries real costs. It needs a publicly reachable webhook. The voice plugin runs inside the Gateway process and occupies an exclusive slot. The repo also has more than 9,000 open issues, so a fork means owning a very large surface ([OpenClaw docs](https://docs.openclaw.ai/plugins/voice-call)). I could not verify from the READMEs whether its long-term memory carries into voice calls. Treat that as unknown.

Every other project supplies one component. The table below sorts the most relevant ones by role, using GitHub data as of 3 October 2026.

|---|---|---|---|
| Whole-agent base | [OpenClaw](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw) | MIT, very active | Fork or mine for heartbeat, voice plugin and skills |
| Voice pipeline | [Pipecat](https://github.com/pipecat-ai/pipecat), [LiveKit Agents](https://github.com/livekit/agents) | BSD-2 / Apache-2.0, active | Build the call path on one of these |

Bolna deserves attention for an Indian build. It supports inbound and outbound calls in English, Hindi and Hinglish, and integrates Twilio, Plivo and Exotel through JSON agent definitions ([Bolna](https://www.bolna.ai/); [Bolna Exotel docs](https://www.bolna.ai/docs/exotel)). The only open-source design found that does daily outbound check-ins is a zero-star hackathon project, an elder-care bot that makes daily calls with GPT-4o, LangGraph and Twilio ([Saathi_AI](https://github.com/anwesha-bose/Saathi_AI)). Nothing in open source implements event-based follow-ups like the dentist call. That is the piece you will write yourself, and it is what will make the product stand out.

Commercial products confirm the gap and offer design lessons. Startups do make real phone calls, but for narrow uses. Dume.ai claims two-way calling for morning briefings, though that claim comes from its own blog ([Dume.ai](https://www.dume.ai/blog/we-tested-top-18-ai-voice-assistants-results-were-unexpected)). Coach Call AI does WhatsApp check-ins and accountability phone calls ([coachcall.ai](https://coachcall.ai/)). The big assistants do not phone you. OpenAI's ChatGPT Pulse produced proactive morning briefings from memory, Gmail and Calendar ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-pulse/)). OpenAI then retired it in mid-2026 in favour of user-defined scheduled tasks, which it called "faster, more reliable and easier to manage" ([Digit](https://www.digit.in/news/general/openai-is-retiring-chatgpt-pulse-and-replacing-it-with-scheduled-tasks-here-is-why.html)). Users complained that Pulse "still latches onto questions i have resolved weeks ago" ([prowlo.com](https://prowlo.com/blog/chatgpt-pulse-shut-down), an aggregator). The memory-heavy companion Dot shut down ([Cryptopolitan](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/dot-personalized-ai-shutting-down/)), and Limitless stopped selling after Meta acquired it ([AI Business](https://aibusiness.com/speech-recognition/meta-acquires-limitless)). Taken together, these failures point to three lessons: let the user set explicit schedules, mark topics as resolved so the assistant stops raising them, and self-host your memory so it cannot disappear in an acquisition.

## Memory needs four layers, and the follow-up layer must be code, not prompting

The memory field has split into four approaches. Extracted-fact layers such as Mem0 are one. Temporal knowledge graphs such as Graphiti/Zep and Cognee are another. In agent-edited memory, used by Letta/MemGPT and Claude's memory tool, the agent rewrites its own memory. Context compression, as in Mastra's Observational Memory and ChatGPT's pre-loaded summary, is the fourth ([Mnemoverse Q3 2026](https://mnemoverse.com/docs/library/ai-memory-solutions-2026-q3)). Graphiti matters most for Jarvis. It is a **bi-temporal graph**: it records both when something happened and when the system learned it. When a new fact contradicts an old one, it marks the old edge invalid instead of deleting it. That means it can answer "what was true on date X", not just "what is current" ([Zep paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13956)). Mem0 is the easier drop-in. Its April 2026 revision changed to add-only extraction combined with semantic, BM25 and entity retrieval, so contradictions now get resolved at ranking time ([Mem0 blog](https://mem0.ai/blog/ai-memory-benchmarks-in-2026)). For a personal assistant that must know the *current* truth (you moved city, changed jobs, a friend's number changed), store explicit supersession metadata whichever library you choose.

Don't pick a library by its benchmark scores. Mem0 self-reports 94.4% on LongMemEval, but a competitor reproduced it at 73.8% ([Mnemoverse](https://mnemoverse.com/docs/library/ai-memory-solutions-2026-q3)). About 6.4% of LoCoMo's answer key is corrupted, and its GPT-4o-mini judge accepted 62.81% of answers that were deliberately wrong but on topic. A plain filesystem agent using grep scored 74% on LoCoMo, beating Mem0's originally reported numbers ([Bloo-mind](https://essays.bloo-mind.ai/posts/2026-05-20-mem-eval/)). The practical takeaway is to build a private eval from synthetic histories spanning several months. It should test knowledge updates, temporal questions, abstention ("I don't know") and follow-ups, and always include a full-context or grep baseline.

The recommended design has four layers. The first is a short **core profile**: name, family, preferences, how you like to be addressed, and your call windows. It is always in context, and a background "sleep-time" job maintains it, the way Letta rewrites memory blocks during idle time ([Letta](https://www.letta.com/blog/sleep-time-compute/)). The second is an **episodic log of dated observations**. Mastra stores three dates on each observation: when it was observed, the date it refers to, and the relative phrase used. A reflector process merges and prunes the log. That approach reaches 94.87% on LongMemEval and compresses well enough for prompt caching ([Mastra](https://mastra.ai/research/observational-memory)). The third is an **entity graph** of people, places and relationships with validity windows (Graphiti). The fourth is a **prospective-memory store**. For ranking, the Generative Agents formula of recency decay plus LLM-rated importance plus relevance remains a sound default ([Generative Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442)). Apply decay to moods and passing states only. A birthday mentioned once stays valuable forever.

Prospective memory is the core of "Jarvis". **PM-Bench (July 2026)** simulates a virtual week with time-based and event-based cues. The best generic scaffold reached only **65.1% F1**. The main failure was calibration: the agent either fired too early or too often, or forgot. Rescheduled tasks scored 0–16.7% ([PM-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12385v1)). The **Prospective Intention Store (September 2026)** keeps each intention's lifecycle in code and lets the model handle only the language. That raised DeepSeek-Chat to **82.9% Set-F1** and a 2B-parameter Gemma from 4.2% to 66.2% ([arXiv 2609.01272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01272)). In practice, when you say "Friday I'm going to the dentist", the extractor writes a typed `intentions` row containing:

- the raw text and the time you said it;
- the resolved absolute date in IST (resolved against when you said it, not when it was stored);
- a confidence score;
- a type (event, promise, reminder or follow-up);
- a status that moves planned → happened, cancelled or rescheduled;
- a follow-up hook such as "due_at + 6h".

Deterministic code then schedules the follow-up call, and the LLM writes only the wording. Intentions with low confidence get a quick confirmation on the next call ("Should I check in after your dentist visit Friday?") so the assistant doesn't over-fire.

Latency decides where memory sits in the call. Casual conversation needs a total response time under one second. Pre-loaded context costs nothing per turn, a semantic search adds 50–200 ms, and asynchronous writes add nothing ([Mem0: voice memory](https://mem0.ai/blog/ai-memory-for-voice-agents)). Mem0's full 2026 pipeline, by contrast, has a p50 of about 1 second ([Mem0 blog](https://mem0.ai/blog/ai-memory-benchmarks-in-2026)). So assemble each call's context *before* dialling: the profile, the last call's summary, intentions that are due, the people relevant today, and the briefing items. Run a targeted search only when the topic shifts, and write new memories after each turn without blocking the conversation.

## The voice stack: cascaded Sarvam + Plivo in Mumbai at about ₹1.5–3 per minute

Speech-to-speech models are now cheap. Gemini 3.8 Live costs about $0.023/min for audio in and out combined ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)), and gpt-realtime-2.1 about $0.096/min (third-party figure; [Synthorai](https://synthorai.io/blog/gpt-realtime-api-pricing/)). But they tie you to the model vendor's voices and to that vendor as the brain. A **cascaded pipeline** (separate STT, LLM and TTS) is the better choice for a Hinglish persona with an Indian voice and a model you choose. Use Pipecat for the widest range of provider plugins. Use LiveKit if you want native SIP and a single WebRTC stack shared by phone and app ([Plivo blog](https://www.plivo.com/blog/how-to-build-a-voice-ai-agent-livekit-pipecat-ten-or-native/)). Either way, add **Pipecat Smart Turn v3**. It is an open, 8 MB model that detects end of turn from the audio itself, runs in about 12 ms on a CPU, and supports Hindi, Marathi and Bengali ([Daily](https://www.daily.co/blog/announcing-smart-turn-v3-with-cpu-inference-in-just-12ms/)). It stops the agent from cutting you off during the mid-sentence "matlab…" pauses that silence-based detection gets wrong.

| Layer | Primary pick | Why / cost | Fallback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speech-to-text (STT) | Sarvam Saaras v3 streaming | 11.47% WER on CoSHE-500 Hinglish vs 12.43% for ElevenLabs Scribe v2 ([benchmarklist](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/asr_code_switch_benchmark/)); ₹30/hr ([Sarvam](https://www.sarvam.ai/api-pricing)) | Scribe v2 realtime, Deepgram |
| Text-to-speech (TTS) | Sarvam Bulbul v3 (v4 "emotional" reported) | Native Hinglish and Indian-English voices, handles numbers natively ([invideo](https://invideo.io/blog/sarvam-bulbul-indian-tts/)); ₹30 per 10k chars, about ₹0.45/min | Cartesia Sonic-3.5 (about 40–90 ms to first audio), ElevenLabs |
| LLM | Fast tier: Claude Haiku-class, Gemini Flash or GPT-mini, streamed with a cached prompt | The LLM's time to first token dominates turn latency ([futureagi](https://futureagi.com/blog/best-voice-ai-june-2026/)) | Larger model for overnight briefing prep |
| Telephony | Plivo India number | ₹0.38/min outbound to Indian mobiles, ₹200/month number, KYC required ([Plivo](https://www.plivo.com/voice/pricing/in/)) | Exotel; Twilio is 2–3x the cost for India |
| In-app call | VoIP push → CallKit/ConnectionService → LiveKit WebRTC | Wideband Opus audio, no PSTN cost | PSTN when there is no data connection |

Plan for **500–800 ms from the moment you stop speaking to the first audio of the reply**. Above 800 ms, "nothing else matters" ([dev.to](https://dev.to/kenimo49/your-voice-agent-has-300ms-before-users-bail-the-three-latency-cliffs-that-kill-voice-ux-416c)). A reference budget is about 250 ms for STT, 150 ms for the LLM, 90 ms for TTS and 50 ms for orchestration, about 540 ms in total ([futureagi](https://futureagi.com/blog/best-voice-ai-june-2026/)). Poor SIP routing alone can add 150–400 ms ([caller.digital](https://caller.digital/blog/telephony-partner-voice-ai-india-plivo-exotel-ozonetel-knowlarity-twilio-2026)). Host the agent in Mumbai, next to Plivo's media and Sarvam. Stream LLM output into TTS sentence by sentence, and start generating early when the end-of-turn detector signals that you are probably done. The weak link is reaching a US-hosted LLM from India. No measured India-to-US round-trip times were found, so measure TTFT from Mumbai before committing to a model.

At personal volume the component costs are trivial: STT about ₹0.5/min, TTS about ₹0.45/min, Plivo ₹0.38/min, plus LLM tokens. That totals roughly ₹1.5–3/min, or a few hundred rupees for 300 minutes a month. Managed platforms add a fee per minute on top: Vapi $0.05/min with your own provider keys ([autocalls.ai](https://autocalls.ai/article/retell-ai-vs-vapi)), Bolna 6¢/min ([Bolna pricing](https://www.bolna.ai/pricing)). That is about $15–25 a month at personal volume. They are a reasonable way to prototype in the first month, but they limit how much you can customise memory and the persona.

The Indian rules are manageable but unresolved. TRAI's amended TCCCPR rules reserve the 140 number series for promotional calls and the 160 series for service and transactional calls, and require DLT registration for commercial communications ([Acefone](https://www.acefone.com/blog/all-about-160-number-series/); [Sigma Chambers](https://www.sigmachambers.in/post/2025-tcccpr-amendments-a-renewed-push-by-trai-for-order-in-commercial-communications-1)). No TRAI text addresses an assistant that calls its own consenting owner. That is probably outside "commercial communication", but treat this as an inference, not legal advice. The practical risk is that Truecaller or your carrier labels the number as spam, so save it as a contact named "Jarvis". If this becomes a product, take legal advice on 160-series and DLT requirements. WhatsApp Business calling costs about 1¢/min in India and bills only answered calls ([richautomate](https://richautomate.in/blog/whatsapp-business-calling-api-india-2026-implementation-guide)), but it needs a Business account and a BSP. For one user, the best experience is an in-app VoIP "call" with PSTN as the fallback.

## Proactivity works best as fixed schedules plus a few precise triggers

Research consistently finds that deciding *when* to step in is the hard part. Fine-tuning on ProactiveBench reached only 66.47% F1 ([ProAgentBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.04482)). A field study found that suggestions delivered at natural boundaries got the most engagement, while interruptions mid-task were dismissed ([arXiv 2601.10253](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10253v1)). Jarvis should therefore not "decide to call you". It should run on **user-chosen slots**: a morning briefing at, say, 7:45 IST, and an evening window around 20:00 for night-before alerts and follow-ups. A small set of high-precision triggers can break the schedule: a flight time change, a cancelled meeting, or an intention that has come due. Durable execution is the right backbone. Temporal splits work into replayable workflows and retrying activities, and its Schedules can start "nudge" workflows for exactly this kind of ambient agent ([Temporal](https://temporal.io/blog/orchestrating-ambient-agents-with-temporal)). Pydantic AI and the OpenAI Agents SDK both integrate with Temporal ([Pydantic AI docs](https://pydantic.dev/docs/ai/integrations/durable_execution/temporal/); [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/09/temporal-aiagent)). Each flight or appointment becomes a workflow that sleeps until "T minus X". When it wakes, it **re-checks the source** for changes before calling you to say "sir, tomorrow you have a flight". Briefings are pre-computed overnight, as Pulse did, so the call starts immediately. If you don't answer, the workflow escalates: a retry, then a WhatsApp or Telegram text summary, then a second call only for urgent items.

Email extraction should be hybrid. First apply a Gmail pre-filter: airlines, IRCTC, banks, couriers and billers, plus keywords such as "PNR" and "due date". Then parse schema.org `FlightReservation` JSON-LD where it exists, since Gmail itself uses that markup for check-in buttons ([Google Developers](https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/markup/reference/flight-reservation)). Fall back to LLM extraction into typed schemas (Flight, Train, Appointment, Bill, Delivery, Commitment), with confidence scores and the source message ID. It is unconfirmed whether IndiGo, Air India or IRCTC embed this markup, so test against your own inbox. For the spoken briefing, cap it at the top three to five items, ranked by sender relationship, deadlines and amounts.

The integration constraints are concrete. **Google Calendar scopes are only "sensitive", but reading Gmail content needs "restricted" scopes.** Publishing to other users then requires verification and an annual CASA security assessment, which takes about 6 weeks and costs anywhere from about $500 to several thousand dollars a year ([Agentic Fabriq](https://www.agenticfabriq.com/blog/google-oauth-verification-casa); [Deepstrike](https://deepstrike.io/blog/google-casa-security-assessment-2025)). Google's OAuth "Testing" mode expires refresh tokens every 7 days, which breaks an always-on agent ([Agentic Fabriq](https://www.agenticfabriq.com/blog/google-oauth-verification-casa)). For a personal build, use an unverified app set to "In production" (allowed for under 100 users) or an Internal Workspace app, and confirm the token behaviour before relying on it. Google now runs official remote MCP servers for Gmail and Calendar in Developer Preview ([Google](https://developers.google.com/workspace/guides/configure-mcp-servers)). Even so, expose **narrow tools** such as `get_today_events` rather than the whole API.

WhatsApp has a policy trap. From 15 January 2026, Meta's Business API **bans general-purpose AI assistants**, while structured bots for notifications remain allowed ([respond.io](https://respond.io/blog/whatsapp-general-purpose-chatbots-ban)). Utility templates cost about ₹0.115 per message in India, and from 1 October 2026 replies inside the 24-hour window are charged too ([Flowcall](https://www.flowcall.co/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing)). Use WhatsApp for templated briefing summaries and reminders, and use Telegram or your own app for free-form chat. For money tracking, Google Play blocks READ_SMS for parsing financial SMS ([Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10208820?hl=en)). Parse bank alert *emails* instead, or sideload a private companion app.

## Privacy and security: your inbox can attack your assistant

An agent that reads email and can also call, message or browse has all three legs of the "lethal trifecta": private data, untrusted content and an outbound channel ([AgentPatterns.ai](https://agentpatterns.ai/security/lethal-trifecta-threat-model/)). This is already happening. In ShadowLeak, hidden text in an email made ChatGPT Deep Research send inbox data out through a URL ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2025/09/shadowleak-zero-click-flaw-leaks-gmail.html)). CVE-2025-46059 exfiltrated email through LangChain's GmailToolkit ([ATR](https://agentthreatrule.org/en/rules/ATR-2026-01964)). Hidden instructions also made Gemini's email summaries show phishing content ([Security Boulevard](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/01/google-gemini-ai-flaw-could-lead-to-gmail-compromise-phishing-2/)).

Filtering out malicious text will not stop these attacks. The fix is architectural, along the lines of the Dual-LLM and CaMeL patterns. CaMeL solved 77% of AgentDojo tasks with provable security by tracking where each value came from ([Zivis](https://www.zivis.ai/articles/lethal-trifecta-dual-llm-camel)). In Jarvis, that means:

- A quarantined model with no tools reads email, after hidden and zero-width text has been stripped, and returns only schema-bound JSON.
- The voice agent sees summaries, never raw email.
- No web-fetch tool shares a context with private data.
- Every outbound action, such as sending a reply, RSVPing or messaging someone, is read back aloud and needs your confirmation.
- Jarvis never reads out a phone number or link from an email as an instruction unless the sender passes SPF/DKIM checks.
- Jarvis only places calls outbound to your registered number, so a spoofed caller ID or cloned voice cannot issue it commands.

For data protection, store OAuth tokens under KMS envelope encryption, encrypt transcripts at rest, keep raw audio and transcripts for about 30 days while keeping summaries longer, and use LLM providers with zero-retention terms. Google's Limited Use policy effectively requires the last point ([Agentic Fabriq](https://www.agenticfabriq.com/blog/google-oauth-verification-casa)). Make memory **visible and editable**. Each item should show its source sentence and date. "Forget that" must delete the item everywhere it has spread: embeddings, graph edges and derived summaries. ChatGPT's own split illustrates the transparency gap: saved memories can be edited, but chat-history memory can only be switched on or off ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/memory-and-new-controls-for-chatgpt/)). If Jarvis becomes a product, India's DPDP Rules apply in full from **13 May 2027**: notice and consent, erasure rights, breach reporting and security safeguards ([PIB](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)). Whether a purely personal build falls under the Act's personal and domestic exemption was not verified.

## Personal without being creepy: a warm voice that shows where it got things

The line between "attentive" and "creepy" is well documented. A CHI'26 study of ChatGPT memory found "relational gains, privacy strains" ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3791635)). Remembering a preference reads as attentive. Mentioning your stress or late-night habits reads as intrusive, and opaque inferences trigger avoidance ([Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1934857/xml)). The Friend pendant drew backlash for "creepy unsolicited advice" and for forgetting details mid-conversation ([WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/friend-ai-necklace-sparks-backlash-for-privacy-woes-and-tech-flaws/)).

Five persona rules follow from this:

- **Cite sources** when raising something ("from yesterday's IndiGo email…", "you mentioned on Monday…").
- **Follow up only on things you said you would do.** Don't follow up on guesses.
- **Keep mood, health and money inferences opt-in** by category.
- **Open each call with a one-line headline** ("three things today, one urgent") and let you interrupt.
- **Offer "stop mentioning this", snooze and skip** on every call.

Start conservative and widen proactivity based on thumbs-up/down feedback, the curation loop Pulse used ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-pulse/)).

Within those limits, several further use cases make Jarvis feel personal, and the data sources above already support them. **Travel concierge**: train PNRs and flights from email, leave-by times, web check-in links from the `checkinUrl` field, and a "landed safely?" follow-up. **Money**: bill due dates and large debits parsed from bank alert emails. **Deliveries**: "your Amazon parcel is out for delivery". **People memory**: birthdays, anniversaries, and "you said you'd call your mother back" commitments, drawn from the entity graph. **Gentle wellbeing check-ins**: a "how did you sleep?" or "how was the interview?" thread, only if you opt in, with mood logged as a decaying observation. **Commitments made on calls**: "remind me Tuesday to call the bank" becomes a durable timer. **A weekly Sunday review** of the week ahead and loops still open. Calendar invites from others count as untrusted input, the same as email ([Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/2025/09/15/gmail-ai-prompt-injection-attack/)).

## Recommended architecture and a five-phase roadmap

The system has two loops that share one memory. The **offline loop** runs on Temporal or a Postgres queue. It ingests Gmail and Calendar, extracts typed items in quarantine, updates memory, pre-computes briefings, and schedules every call. The **live loop** is a Pipecat or LiveKit call session. When a call starts, it loads a pre-assembled context pack. During the call it uses a fast streaming LLM with a few narrow tools, and it writes new memories and intentions after each turn without blocking the conversation.

```
 Gmail/Calendar ─▶ Ingest workers ─▶ Quarantined extractor (no tools, JSON only)
                                          │
                                          ▼
   ┌───────────── Memory service (Postgres + pgvector + Graphiti) ─────────────┐
   │ core profile │ episodic log (3 dates) │ people graph │ INTENTIONS table   │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
          ▲  async writes                     │ due intentions / items
          │                                   ▼
   Live call (Mumbai)                 Scheduler (Temporal): briefing 07:45,
   Plivo/WebRTC ─▶ Smart Turn ─▶      T-minus alerts, follow-ups, retries,
   Sarvam STT ─▶ fast LLM ─▶ Bulbul   escalation ─▶ "dial + context pack"
          │
          ▼
   Confirm-gated actions (send/RSVP) · Langfuse traces · WhatsApp/Telegram fallback
```

| Phase | Weeks | Deliverable | Exit criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Spike | 1–2 | Pipecat or LiveKit + Sarvam + Plivo outbound call to your phone with a fixed script; alternatively prototype on Bolna or Vapi | Response latency under 800 ms (p50) measured from Mumbai; Hinglish heard correctly |
| 1. Morning briefing | 3–5 | Calendar + Gmail ingestion (personal OAuth app), quarantined extraction, overnight briefing precompute, scheduled 07:45 call, WhatsApp/Telegram fallback | Every briefing item traceable to its source; zero hallucinated events on a golden set |
| 2. Memory | 6–9 | Core profile + episodic log + people graph; editable memory web view; "what do you know about…?" and "forget that" | Private eval over months of synthetic history beats the grep/full-context baseline |
| 3. Prospective memory | 9–12 | Intentions store and state machine; night-before alerts that re-check sources; Friday-style follow-ups; confirmation for low-confidence intentions | Precision/recall tracked; under 1 unwanted call per week |
| 4. Polish | 12–16 | In-app VoIP call (CallKit/ConnectionService), Bulbul v4 or tuned voice persona, interruption handling, Langfuse tracing, nightly red-team suite of malicious emails | p95 turn latency and answer/hang-up rates on a dashboard; injection suite passes |
| 5. Productize (optional) | 16+ | Gmail restricted-scope verification + CASA, DPDP notice/consent/erasure, per-user KMS keys, telecom legal review | Compliance before 13 May 2027 |

Instrument from the first phase. ## Conclusion

The defining feature of Jarvis is not the voice or the model, which are now commodity parts costing a few rupees a minute. It is **reliable prospective memory paired with restraint**. Benchmarks show that LLMs are good at recalling the past but poor at acting at the right future moment unless code holds the state. OpenAI's retreat from Pulse to scheduled tasks shows that people prefer an assistant whose proactivity is predictable. The winning design is therefore deliberately unglamorous: deterministic schedules and intention state machines, with the LLM confined to extracting and phrasing. The LLM still makes Jarvis feel human, and the deterministic parts make it trustworthy.

Two risks deserve more attention than they usually get. The first is your own inbox as an attack vector. Wall it off architecturally on day one, because filtering out malicious text will not stop these attacks. The second is the regulatory and platform gap: TRAI's silence on calls an assistant places to its own consenting owner, Meta's ban on general-purpose assistants on WhatsApp, and Gmail's restricted-scope regime. None of these blocks a personal build. All of them shape what a product version can become, so keep the channels swappable and the data self-hosted from the start.
